About Me

I am Rayilla Sai Prakash, an early-career researcher from Hyderabad, Telangana, India, with an interdisciplinary background in Linguistics and Geo-Informatics.

My research focuses on Telangana Telugu, particularly regional dialect variation, sociolinguistics, language documentation, lexical resources, oral traditions, language attitudes, and the relationship between language and identity. My Geo-Informatics background also informs my interest in spatial approaches to language and community research.

My Journey into Linguistics

My interest in Telangana Telugu began with a personal experience during my school years. In the seventh grade, while studying at a residential school, my parents visited me. During a conversation in the school office, my mother naturally spoke to me in our home variety of Telangana Telugu. Some members of the school staff laughed after hearing her speak. Nothing was directed at me personally, but the moment stayed with me because it was the first time I became aware that the language spoken in my home and community could be perceived differently from other varieties of Telugu.

In the days that followed, I decided that I would continue speaking my own variety of Telangana Telugu rather than changing it to fit other people's expectations. I have continued to use my native variety, including when communicating with speakers of other regional varieties of Telugu.

Years later, studying linguistics gave me the concepts to understand that experience. I came to recognise questions of language attitudes, dialect prestige, language variation, and identity as sociolinguistic questions rather than judgments about linguistic quality. That shift from personal experience to academic inquiry became one of the motivations behind my research.

My academic path has also been interdisciplinary. My undergraduate training was in Electronics and Communication Engineering. During that period, I became interested in applying technical knowledge to practical problems, including an undergraduate project idea for a sensor-based irrigation system intended to reduce the need for farmers to repeatedly operate irrigation pumps when wells ran dry. Although the project ultimately took a different direction, the experience reinforced an interest in research grounded in real-world problems.

I subsequently pursued an M.Sc. in Geo-Informatics at Osmania University. During my postgraduate studies, a linguistics elective introduced me to the scientific study of language and its relationship with society. That experience led me to pursue an M.A. in Linguistics, where my research turned increasingly toward regional variation in Telangana Telugu.

Today, I see these stages as connected rather than separate. Engineering taught me to approach practical problems systematically; Geo-Informatics gave me tools for spatial and environmental analysis; and Linguistics gave me a framework for studying language, society, and identity. I now bring these perspectives together in my work on Telangana Telugu and related community-based documentation.

Research Direction

My long-term goal is to contribute to the scholarly documentation and digital preservation of Telangana's linguistic diversity. I am particularly interested in regional variation, language maintenance, multilingualism, language attitudes, and the ways migration and social change affect Telangana Telugu across communities and generations.
