Putthentikalu (Ritual Tonsure / First Haircutting Ceremony)

👤 Vajramma🎂 Age 80💼 Agricultural Labourer📍 Jagtial district, Telangana

Speaker Metadata

Vajramma, age 80 · Agricultural Labourer — Jagtial district, Telangana.

Original Telangana Narrative

పస్టు పుట్టేన్టికలు పెట్టుకొని, పోశవ్వ తల్లి కాడ బోనందీసి ఎములాడ దేవుని దగ్గరికి మొక్కులున్నోల్లు ఏం చేస్తురంటే ఇంత గడ్డి వెట్టుకొని ఎడ్లబండ్లె, ఉప్పు పప్పు బోళ్ళు బొకేలు బట్టలు సదురుకొని పొద్దూక పొయ్యి అయ్యగారింటి కాడ నిద్రయేసి పొద్దుగాల మబ్బుల ముడిటికి లేశి గుల్లెకి పొయ్య మోక్కులు అప్పయేప్పి బెల్లం పంచి, బద్ది పోశమ్మ కాడ కోన్ని కోసి బోనం అండుకొని తిని తాగి ఇంటికత్తురు.

English Translation

First, the birth-hair (Putthentikalu) is clipped, and Bonam (a ritual food offering) is offered at Mother Poshamma’s shrine. Those who have pending vows to fulfill at the Vemulawada temple (Emulaada Devudu) pack a bundle of grass into their bullock carts, along with salt, lentils, cooking vessels (Bollu Bokeelu), and clothes. Traveling until dusk (Poddooka), they stay the night at the priest’s (Ayyagaaru) house. Waking up in the early hours before dawn (Mabbula Muditiki), around three o'clock, they proceed to the temple, fulfill their vows (Mukkulu Appaeppi), and distribute jaggery. Then, at the Baddi Poshamma shrine, they sacrifice a goat/fowl (Koni Kosi), cook the Bonam, eat, drink, and return home.

Festival Practices

Child's first ritual haircut; offering cooked rice pot (Bonam) to the local goddess Poshamma; packing bullock carts with self-sustaining provisions (fodder, salt, pulses, cooking utensils); nocturnal lodging at a Brahmin priest's house (Ayyagaari illu); pre-dawn temple darshan at 3:00 AM; distribution of jaggery (Bellam) as prasadam; animal sacrifice (Koni Kosi) at the peripheral shrine of Baddi Poshamma followed by a sacrificial feast.

Festival Vocabulary

TermMeaning
Bollu Bokeelu (బోళ్ళు బొకేలు)A specific regional double-noun referring comprehensively to kitchen utensils and brass/earthen pots required for outdoor camp cooking.
Poddooka (పొద్దూక)Sunset / the precise hour when the sun drops below the horizon.
Mabbula Muditiki (మబ్బుల ముడిటికి)Literally "the third hour in the clouds," meaning 3:00 AM, capturing the traditional pre-modern tracking of nocturnal time.
Mokkulu Appaeppi (మోక్కులు అప్పఎప్పి)Literally "surrendering or returning the vows," a distinct transactional expression for fulfilling a debt owed to a deity.
Baddi Poshamma (బద్దీ పోశమ్మ)A peripheral village deity shrine located near the boundaries of the major Sanskritized Shaivite temple complex at Vemulawada.

Linguistic & Ethnographic Commentary

Cultural Interpretation

Vajramma’s account highlights the syncretic, multi-layered religious universe of agrarian subalterns. The pilgrimage bridges two vastly different religious traditions: the Sanskritized temple of Emulaada (Sri Raja Rajeshwara Swamy, a form of Shiva) and the localized, non-Sanskritized village goddess Poshamma. For an agricultural laborer, fulfilling a vow is structured as a literal transactional settlement (Mukkulu Appaeppi). The inclusion of "a bunch of grass in the carts" showcases an intimate multi-species care system where the working bullocks' nutrition is prioritized alongside the family's provisions during travel.

Variation & Change

This narrative archives a completely vanished travel infrastructure. The meticulous packing of salt, pulses, and kitchen vessels highlights an era of total self-reliance during travel, necessitated by a lack of commercial hotels and caste-segregated dining options. Staying at the Ayyagaaru (priest) house implies a traditional patron-client network (Jajmani-adjacent structures) that has largely disintegrated in contemporary commercialized temple tourism.

Research Note

This text is a prime example of folk cartographic memory. It details how marginal communities navigated sacred landscapes by relying on spatial checkpoints (traveling till Poddooka, sleeping at the priest’s house, entering the temple at Muditiki). It provides excellent raw data for a doctoral dissertation tracking the intersection of subaltern mobility, domestic labor, and non-Brahminical ritual synthesis.
