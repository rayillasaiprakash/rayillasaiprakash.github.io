Siri Dhanya Millet Cultivation and Water Sustainability in Telangana

Field observations, community knowledge, published water-footprint evidence, and a proposed GIS direction

Rayilla Sai Prakash · M.Sc. Geo-Informatics · Jagtial–Karimnagar region, Telangana

Open Current Working Paper (PDF)

About the project

This is an ongoing independent study of Siri Dhanya (minor millet) cultivation in Telangana. The current manuscript brings together field-reported cultivation practices and water-use estimates from the Jagtial–Karimnagar region with published water-footprint literature and a source-based nutrient comparison. A later stage of the project proposes a GIS framework for examining where millet cultivation could be investigated further in relation to water stress, rainfall and crop patterns.

Study region

Jagtial–Karimnagar, Telangana
Field-informed observations from an agricultural region of Telangana.

Millets documented

Foxtail · Barnyard · Kodo · Little · Browntop
The manuscript discusses these minor millets within the Siri Dhanya classification used in the cited source material.

Research status

Work in progress
Field estimates and source-based comparisons are being documented before further validation and spatial analysis.

Evidence and scope: The local water-use and yield figures are community-held, practice-based estimates, not instrumented field-trial measurements. The nutrient table reproduces values from the cited Siridhanya source and is not an independent laboratory analysis. These distinctions are retained deliberately while the research is in development.

Research questions and aims

  • How do local farmers describe the cultivation calendar, rainfall needs and water requirements of minor millets?
  • How do these field-reported observations compare with published water-footprint evidence for crops such as paddy, wheat and other cereals?
  • What nutrient-composition information is available in the cited Siridhanya source, and how can it be documented without presenting source claims as independent laboratory findings?
  • How could GIS be used in a later phase to examine relationships among rainfall, groundwater stress and crop distribution in Telangana?

Current evidence

Field-reported agronomic observations

Arikalu (kodo millet)

120-day duration; critical rainfall reported at germination and panicle formation; approximately 250 L/kg grain reported locally; 12–13 qtl/acre reported yield.

Korralu (foxtail millet)

75–90-day duration; reported as tolerant of dry spells; 7–9 qtl/acre reported yield.

Andukorralu (browntop millet)

60–70-day duration; minimal water reported; up to three crop cycles per year reported; 8–10 qtl/acre reported yield.

Samalu (little millet)

Approximately 90-day duration; minimal water requirement reported; 6–7 qtl/acre reported yield.

Important limitation: The manuscript notes that published water-footprint literature does not separately provide species-level figures for the specific minor millets in the local Siri Dhanya classification. The approximately 250 L/kg kodo figure therefore remains a field-reported estimate requiring independent agronomic validation.

Proposed next phase

The GIS component is currently a proposed direction rather than a completed spatial analysis. The planned work would investigate the feasibility of combining groundwater-stress classifications, district-level crop-area data and rainfall information to construct a transparent suitability framework for further millet research in Telangana.

Working paper

The current four-page document contains the introduction, source-based nutrient table, field-reported water comparison, literature comparison and references. It is intentionally presented as a manuscript in development, not as a finished peer-reviewed publication.
