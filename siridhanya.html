Siri Dhanya Millet Cultivation and Water Sustainability in Telangana

Field observations, community knowledge, source-based nutrient information, published water-footprint evidence, and a proposed GIS direction.

Rayilla Sai Prakash · M.Sc. Geo-Informatics · Jagtial–Karimnagar region, Telangana

Open Current Working Paper (PDF)
WORK IN PROGRESS

About the project

This independent study examines Siri Dhanya (minor millet) cultivation in Telangana. The current stage brings together field-reported cultivation practices and water-use estimates from the Jagtial–Karimnagar region with published water-footprint literature and source-based nutrient information. A later phase proposes a GIS framework for examining rainfall, groundwater stress and crop patterns.

Research focus

Study region

Jagtial–Karimnagar, Telangana
Field-informed observations from an agricultural region of Telangana.

Millets documented

Foxtail · Barnyard · Kodo · Little · Browntop
Discussed within the Siri Dhanya classification used in the cited source material.

Current stage

Documentation and comparison
Field estimates and source-based evidence are being assembled before further validation and spatial analysis.

Research questions and aims

  • How do local farmers describe the cultivation calendar, rainfall needs and water requirements of minor millets?
  • How do field-reported observations compare with published water-footprint evidence?
  • What nutrient-composition information is available in the cited Siridhanya source, and how can it be documented without presenting source claims as independent laboratory findings?
  • How could GIS be used in a later phase to examine relationships among rainfall, groundwater stress and crop distribution?

Current evidence

Field-reported agronomic observations

Arikalu (kodo millet)

120-day duration; critical rainfall reported at germination and panicle formation; approximately 250 L/kg grain reported locally; 12–13 qtl/acre reported yield.

Korralu (foxtail millet)

75–90-day duration; reported as tolerant of dry spells; 7–9 qtl/acre reported yield.

Andukorralu (browntop millet)

60–70-day duration; minimal water reported; up to three crop cycles per year reported; 8–10 qtl/acre reported yield.

Samalu (little millet)

Approximately 90-day duration; minimal water requirement reported; 6–7 qtl/acre reported yield.

Current limitations: Local water-use and yield figures are community-held, practice-based estimates, not instrumented field-trial measurements. The nutrient table is reproduced from the cited Siridhanya source. Published literature also does not provide species-level water-footprint figures for the specific minor millets in the local classification.

Proposed next phase

The GIS component is currently a proposed direction rather than a completed spatial analysis. The planned work would investigate the feasibility of combining groundwater-stress classifications, district-level crop-area data and rainfall information to construct a transparent suitability framework for further millet research in Telangana.

Working paper

The current document is a manuscript in development, not a finished peer-reviewed publication. It contains the current introduction, source-based nutrient table, field-reported water comparison, literature comparison and references.
